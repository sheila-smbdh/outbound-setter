These five objections cost us the most bookings: price, plus the top four from our objection study of real setter calls. Each one has a rule, the lines to say, and what never to say. Whatever the objection, the call still ends with a booking made live.

## 1. Price: "How much is it?" / "That's too expensive"

How to handle it:
- Show our fee as part of what it costs to buy any business: the down payment, plus legal and due diligence fees. Never give the number on its own.
- Give the two ends of the range and tie them to the savings. Don't go line by line through legal, DD or the SBA loan rebate. Those depend on the prospect's situation, and the closer covers them.
- Say clearly that it's a range because every situation is different, and that's why they need the second call.
- Make it clear we don't take a cut of their deal. The range comes from their deal's own costs and the savings they qualify for.

What to say:
"Totally fair. It's a range, because it depends on your deal.
- On the high end, some members are about $20K out of pocket.
- On the other end, some actually come out $10-15K ahead once you factor in the due diligence savings.
To be clear, that's not us taking a percentage of your deal. The range comes from things like your financing and what your deal needs on the legal and DD side. I don't want to throw all of that at you in 15 minutes and confuse you.
That's why I'd love to get you on a screenshare with one of our enrollment advisors. They'll plug in your numbers and show you exactly what it looks like for you."

## 2. "Send me some info"

The most damaging objection in the study: only 12.7% of these calls get set.

Rule: Say yes to the email, then book the call live in the same breath. The email is prep for the call, not a replacement for it.
- "Absolutely, I'll send you everything. Let's get the next call on the calendar right now, and I'll send it straight after, so you've got the context before you sit down with the advisor."
- "Want some time to digest it first? I can put you out a week. Friday evening or Saturday morning, which is easier?"
- Create a task for yourself to follow-up

Never: offer the booking link as the easier option. Say "sounds good" to "I'll reach back out." Offer to send info before they ask. List what's in the email.

## 3. "Not right now"

The most common objection in the study, and the one setters mishandle most.

How to handle it:
- First, figure out which kind of "not right now" it is. Is it money or timing? Each one gets handled differently.
- Ask: "Are you already looking at businesses?" If they are, the timing is already now.

If it's timing (life event, busy season, "maybe next year"):
- Ask when they want to own a business, not when they want to start.
- Work backward from that date. The process takes about 8 months from starting the search to closing.
- If they want to own within 6 to 8 months, they need to start now.
- If their start date really is later, don't push. Set the follow-up (see below).

Say this: "Totally get it. Can I ask, when would you ideally want to own a business? ... Got it. So the whole process, from searching to actually closing, usually takes about eight months. If you want to own by [their date], you'd want to start your search around [date minus 8 months]. That means [now / in X months]."

If it's money ("I don't have the cash right now"):
- Don't disqualify yet. Ask whether a HELOC or 401(k) could be part of how they fund the deal.
- If yes, keep the call going and let the enrollment advisor walk through the options. Don't explain how those funding structures work yourself.
- If neither applies and they don't have a realistic way to fund the down payment, disqualify.

Say this: "Totally fair. A lot of our members don't fund the down payment from savings alone. Do you own a home, or have money in a 401(k) or IRA? Some buyers use home equity or retirement funds as part of their down payment."

Before you end the call (if not booking now):
1. Set the correct call outcome in Close. This triggers the right follow-up playbook for that lead.
2. Create a follow-up task in Close for the date you agreed on.
3. Confirm the follow-up time with the lead before hanging up.

Say this: "Let's do this: I'll reach back out around [date] so you're right on track to start. Does [day/time] work for a quick check-in?"

Never: "Absolutely, I think so too." "I'll check in with you periodically" instead of a booked slot. "If we do get to a further call."

## 4. "Is this legit?"

Usually someone who already paid for a program that delivered nothing.

Rule: Agree the space has bad actors, give a real number right away, explain how we get paid, admit what you don't know, then book live.
- "Fair. A lot of companies in this space can't point to a single closed deal. Since we started, we've helped members close over $297M, that's 145 closed deals, with another 314 under LOI right now."
- "On incentives: we don't represent sellers, and we don't take a commission from them. A broker gets paid a percentage by the seller, which tells you who they work for. We work for the buyer."
- "I won't guess at a number I can't back up. The advisor has that data, so I'll flag your question and have them open with it. How does Thursday look?"
- "We have over 50 testimonials from past members, and we share a new one every week. I will send over the link to the testimonials right now." (https://testimonials.smbdealhunter.xyz/)
- Book them live while you are still on the call

Never: send a testimonial library and a booking link instead of answering. Contradict them ("there is a guarantee, yeah, yeah"). Say "it might not be the best fit" because they got burned before.

## 5. "I can do this myself" / "What am I paying for?"

Study ranks 4 and 5 combined. Both come from a prospect who can't yet see what we do that they can't.

How to handle it:
- Don't argue with them or try to prove they can't do it alone. Many of our members could. The question is what it costs them in time and money.
- Focus on two things: savings from our pre-negotiated financial and legal due diligence rates, and access to off-market deals.
- Don't bring up the SBA loan rebate here. Save it for the advisor.

What to say:
"Totally, and a lot of our members are very capable buyers. Here's how I'd think about it. First, just by using the due diligence firms and SBA lenders we partner with, you'd save around $10K-$15K from our pre-negotiated rates.
On top of that, you get access to off-market deals you won't find on the public listing sites.
So even if you don't need our 1:1 advisory support at all, you're still saving money on costs you'd have to pay anyway, and you're seeing more deals than you would on your own.
The enrollment advisor can walk you through exactly how all of this works for your deal. Let's get you on the calendar. Which day and time work best for you this week?"

If they ask why they can't get those rates on their own:
"It comes down to volume. In the past year alone, we've helped members close about $175M in deals, so we get wholesale rates from diligence firms that specialize in your target deal size. A single buyer going to them directly wouldn't get that pricing."

## Quick answers

Short questions that aren't objections. Answer in one line, then keep moving.

| They ask | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "Do you have deals in my industry or area?" | Quick question so we pull the right ones: what area are you looking in, what industries interest you, and roughly what size deal are you targeting? Perfect. Let's get you booked with the advisor so they have time to pull those together. Which day and time work best for you this week? |
| "What if I don't find a business?" | "Great question, and that's exactly why we built our guarantee. If you put in the work and haven't closed within 12 months, we keep working with you 1:1 until you do. That costs us real money every month you haven't closed, so we're very motivated to get you there. I'll give you an extreme example so you can see how serious we are about it. One of our members, Amanda Battat, took 18 months to close. We kept supporting her the whole way through, well past the 12 months, until she got her business. Helen actually just did a full breakdown of her story, and I'll send you that video in my follow-up. Your enrollment advisor can walk you through exactly how the guarantee works. Let's get you booked. Which day and time work best for you this week?" |
| "Who are your members?" | "Mostly corporate and tech professionals, people from M&A, and current business owners buying their next one. We have over 50 testimonials from past members, and we share a new one every week. I will send over the link to the testimonials right now." |

From elsewhere in the same script (used by the rubric): book the enrollment advisor call live while the lead is on the line ("Do not send a Calendly link and hope they book it, because most won't"); use the round robin link, not a specific closer; leads who miss the bar on capital (under $100K including HELOC and 401K) or SBA eligibility (not a U.S. citizen, credit under 680, past federal loan default, probation or parole) go to the redirect path, not a pitch.
