# Objection check: how each call is graded

Source: `SCRIPT-objections.md` (the live "Setter Inbound Call Script_Oct 2026" wins if they differ). Grade what the setter said, not what Close says. Counting and grading rules here change only with Sheila's say-so.

## 1. Which calls are in

- The three setters' calls on the day (ET), any direction, with a recording of **60+ seconds** (Sheila, Oct 25). Shorter calls are not transcribed.
- From the transcript, decide the call type first. Only **live conversations with the lead** are graded:
  - `conversation`: the setter and the lead actually talk about the lead's search.
  - Not graded (counted only): `voicemail` (screener or voicemail greeting, then the setter's message), `screener` (never got through), `logistics` (confirming or moving a meeting already booked, a wrong person, a callback about something else), `unclear` (audio too poor to tell).
- The transcript has no speaker labels. Work out who is speaking from content (the setter opens, asks the qualifying questions and pitches). When you can't tell who said a line that matters for the grade, say "unclear" rather than guessing.

## 2. What counts as an objection

Tag every objection the lead raises, in the lead's own words (short verbatim quote):

| Tag | Lead says something like |
| --- | --- |
| `price` | "How much is it?", "What does it cost?", "That's too expensive", "Is there a fee?" |
| `send_info` | "Send me some info", "Email me something", "I'll look at it and reach back out", "Do you have a website I can check?" |
| `not_now` | "Not right now", "Maybe next year", "Just researching", "Too busy", "I don't have the cash right now" (record `money` or `timing`) |
| `legit` | "Is this legit?", "Is this a scam?", "I got burned before", "Sounds too good to be true" |
| `diy` | "I can do this myself", "What am I paying for?", "Why would I need you?", "I can find deals on BizBuySell" |
| `quick:deals_area`, `quick:no_business`, `quick:members` | the three quick-answer questions |
| `other` | any other pushback (spouse/partner, already working with a broker, bad timing for a call). Quote it and grade only "booked live or a confirmed next step" |

A lead asking "how much" is a price objection even when said calmly. One call can have several objections; grade each.

## 3. Grade per objection

Each objection gets **Hit**, **Partial** or **Miss**, plus the one line that decided it.

- **Hit**: follows the script's rule for that objection and does none of its "Never" items, and the call ends with a live booking attempt (or, for `not_now` timing that is truly later, a confirmed follow-up day and time).
- **Partial**: right instinct, but skips a required piece (for example answers price as a range but never mentions the advisor screenshare, or says yes to info and asks for a time but lets the lead off without one).
- **Miss**: any "Never" item, or the objection ends the booking attempt (setter accepts it and moves to goodbye or a link).

Checklist per tag (what Hit needs):

- `price`: fee framed as a range (about $20K out of pocket on the high end, $10-15K ahead on the other) inside total cost of buying; says it's a range because every deal differs; says we don't take a cut of the deal; points to the advisor screenshare and asks for a time. **Miss** if the setter gives a flat number on its own (for example "$20,000 one time"), or goes line by line through legal, DD or the SBA loan rebate.
- `send_info`: yes to the email AND asks to book in the same breath; offers a week out if they want time to digest. **Miss** if the setter offers the booking link as the easier option, says "sounds good" to "I'll reach back out", offered info before the lead asked, or lists what's in the email.
- `not_now`: asks whether they're already looking at businesses, and works out money vs timing. Timing: asks when they want to *own*, works back about 8 months. Money: asks about home equity, 401(k) or IRA without explaining how those structures work; disqualifies only if no realistic way to fund. If not booking: confirms a specific follow-up day and time on the call. **Miss** on "Absolutely, I think so too", "I'll check in with you periodically" with no slot, or "If we do get to a further call".
- `legit`: agrees the space has bad actors; gives a real number (over $297M, 145 closed deals, 314 under LOI; the $300M figure from the script's opener is fine too); explains we work for the buyer and take no seller commission; admits what they don't know and hands it to the advisor; offers testimonials as well as, not instead of, answering; books live. **Miss** if the setter sends testimonials and a link instead of answering, contradicts the lead, or says "it might not be the best fit" because they were burned.
- `diy`: does not argue; leads with about $10-15K savings from pre-negotiated DD and lender rates, and off-market deal access; asks for a day and time. **Miss** if the setter argues the lead can't do it alone or brings up the SBA loan rebate.
- `quick:*`: answers in a line or two in the script's spirit, then moves to booking.

## 4. Call-level flags (any graded call)

- **Booked live?** yes / no / attempted. Script: book while the lead is on the line, round robin, no "here's my Calendly".
- **Off-script claims** (flag with quote): guarantees beyond the 12-month work-with-you guarantee, comparisons to Goldman Sachs, Harris Williams, Woodbridge or any bank, rolling the fee into the loan, SBA rebates promised on the setter call.
- **Pitched a lead who missed the bar** (under $100K including HELOC and 401K, or an SBA blocker) instead of redirecting.

## 5. Per-setter roll-up

- Conversations graded, and calls skipped by type.
- Objections heard, by tag.
- Handled per script = Hit / all graded objections (Partial counts as half in the %).
- Most common Miss reason, with the best quote, and the script line they should have used.
- One best example (a Hit worth sharing), if any.

Whisper mishears names and brand words ("SMB Deal Hunter" can come out garbled). Don't grade wording that is obviously a transcription error; quote the meaning, and say "(transcript unclear)" where needed.
