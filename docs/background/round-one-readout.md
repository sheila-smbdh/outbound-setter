# Welcome Series Dialing Test: Readout

Sep 28, 2026 · [byline: mention chip for Zain Khan]

Calling Welcome Series sign-ups gets people on the phone: 100 of the 513 leads Bali dialed had a real conversation with him, verified from the call recordings. The weak link is turning those conversations into closer calls: 6 of 100 (6%), about 1 for every 100 leads dialed. Speed and follow-up both help, and both run well below what the SOP asks for, so today's results are a floor for this channel, not its ceiling.

Quick terms: a setter (Bali) calls new leads and books the good ones onto a closer's calendar. A closer runs the sales call for Pro. A "closer call" below means one of those bookings.

## From leads to closer calls

[CHART 1: widgets/1-funnel.jsx | caption: "Close call log and Calendly bookings · Sep 17 to 28, 2026"]

Getting people on the phone is not the problem: about 1 in 5 people Bali dials talks with him. Per call that is 16%, double the 8% his tracker shows. The drop comes after that: 6 of 100 conversations became closer calls. Most conversations are short, with a median of about 1 minute, so what happens on the call is where to look next.

## What it is worth in closer calls

Conservatively, calling every Welcome Series lead the way Bali calls today would book about 7 closer calls a week. That counts only the 5 closer calls that weren't canceled, which works out to about 1 per 100 leads dialed.

| Scenario | New leads a week | Closer calls a week |
|---|---|---|
| Bali today, one setter | about 360 | about 3 |
| Every lead dialed, same execution | 743 | about 7 |
| Upside, not in the plan: every lead called within 15 minutes | 743 | about 12 |

- 743 is the weekly lead count in the test tracker's Projections tab, which forecasts 6 to 11 closer calls a week. Close supports the lower half of that range today.
- One setter can't cover the volume. Bali's own tracker showed 295 leads waiting in his backlog on Sep 25.
- The upside row assumes every lead is reached as often as the 48 leads called within 15 minutes were. That is a small sample, so read it as a direction, not a forecast.
- There is no control group yet. Of the leads Bali dialed but never talked with, 22 still booked a call with another rep. We can't yet say how many of Bali's 6 would have booked anyway.

## Openers and pickups

Bali used essentially one opener on every call that connected:

> "Hey [name], can you hear me okay? Hey, this is Bali. I just saw that you clicked on our site about buying a business. Is that something you're actively pursuing, or just browsing for now?"

His small variations (naming SMB Deal Hunter up front, "you filled out some information," "a few minutes ago") made no difference we can measure to whether a call became a conversation or a booking. That's expected: people decide whether to pick up from the caller ID, before they hear a word. The opener only matters once they answer, and 103 of his 114 pickups became real conversations, so it's already doing its job. Round two drops the "you clicked on our site" line; the call script uses "You asked us to reach out about buying a business" instead.

What moved pickups:

| What we compared | Pickup rate per dial | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Called within 15 minutes of sign-up vs. more than a day later | 26% vs. 15% | Faster calls picked up more; a small sample (57 fast dials) |
| Monday and Friday vs. Tuesday to Thursday | 23% vs. 12% | Too big a gap to be chance, but there are only two of each weekday so far |
| Time of day, in 2-hour blocks from 9am to 9pm Eastern | 12% to 24% | No steady pattern; the gaps are within normal noise |
| First try vs. second and third tries | 19% vs. 15% | Later tries still reach people |

Two pickup problems to fix in round two:

- **The caller ID says Canada.** Bali dials from an 807 area code, which is Ontario. In 4 of the 16 long conversations the lead asked about it, including "The number that you call me from said Canada, are you located in Canada?" Calling from US local numbers is the cheapest pickup test to run.
- **1 in 6 dials hits a call screener.** 106 dials reached an iPhone or Google Voice screener asking who's calling, and 12 got through (11%). All 12 came when Bali gave only his first name ("Hey, it's Bali"). None of the 13 times he added "with SMB Deal Hunter" or the reason got through. The sample is small, so treat it as a test, not a rule: keep the screener answer to a first name.

## How the calls that booked went

Every closer call Bali booked followed the same pattern: a few minutes of questions, a walk-through of the program and the price, then booking the closer himself while the lead was still on the phone. The findings below come from 16 of his longest conversations, transcribed in full: the 9 that led to a booking and 7 that didn't.

[DIAGRAM 5: widgets/5-call-flow.jsx | caption: "Call recordings transcribed in full · 16 conversations, Sep 16 to 28, 2026"]

On almost every booked call he asked:

- How long have you been looking?
- What's behind it: replacing your income, or adding to it?
- What kind of business, and where?
- When do you want to close?
- What do you want it to pay you each year?
- How much can you put down, and is that cash or tied up in a house?

He also asked one lead whether he was a US citizen. That question belongs on every call: since March 2026, SBA loans require every owner to be a US citizen.

| What happened on the call | 9 calls that booked | 7 long calls that didn't |
|---|---|---|
| Bali stated the price | 6 | 5 |
| Bali asked how much cash they could put down | 8 | 5 |
| Lead questioned paying upfront | 1, and still booked | 4, and none booked |
| A meeting within two weeks was set before hanging up | 8 | 0 |

Price came up on both sides, so it doesn't explain the gap. The booking does. When Bali offered a day and time and booked it on the call, the lead booked. When he sent an email, texted a calendar link or asked if they were "open to hearing us out," nobody booked.

Three of the lost leads looked like strong buyers:

- **Tom Deater** has $500,000 in cash, about $10 million in property and stocks, and 40 years in finance. He said "I'm not complaining about the $20,000 fee" and only wanted to see sample deals. Bali explained why upfront fees are normal instead, and logged him as a "tire kicker."
- **Richard Diaz** owns a business, has $250,000 in cash and wants one that makes $700,000 a year. He ended the call seconds after Bali confirmed the fee is paid upfront.
- **Antonio Gabriele** owned dealerships for 40 years and has $250,000 to $500,000 to put down. Four minutes in he said "So are we good? You're going to have your other guy call me." Bali kept pitching and lost him on the fee.

**What Bali does well.** When he's on the phones he calls fast (one lead: "You guys are quick"), gets a real cash number on nearly every call, lets serious leads talk, and on his best calls books the closer before hanging up. That last habit is the one to make standard.

**What he sent after the call.** On booked calls he texted a short video from Helen and client testimonials, and asked for a thumbs-up so the lead's phone saves his number. He emailed 44 of the 100 people he talked with a shared Close template called "Setter Resources - Omar" (subject "SMB Deal Hunter - Info Packet"). Omar Kaddoura created it in January; it links a program overview deck, the advisor team page, sample deals, a CIM review video and testimonials. Of the roughly 410 leads he never reached, 7 in 10 got no text or email at all. The rest got his "just browsing?" text or a Welcome Series email with a calendar link. One lead, reached while on vacation, booked a closer call 4 days later after two follow-up texts.

**Claims to confirm or cut.** Bali's pitch leans on lines leadership hasn't signed off on:

- Comparing SMB Deal Hunter to Goldman Sachs, Harris Williams and Woodbridge ("the same services")
- Transaction volume that changes by call: "$175 million last year," "$175 to $300 million a year," "about $200 million a year"
- "Guarantees in writing" and "guaranteed to work with you until you close"
- SBA lenders paying "a $5,000 or $10,000 rebate" shared with the client, and rolling the fee into the loan
- Deals "nobody's ever touched before." Off-market deals go to every Pro member, so this overstates it
- "8 to 10 months" to close, when the program's own average is about 8
- Not knowing where the company is based: "I'm pretty sure we have an office in New York"

**One conduct issue.** After Richard Diaz ended the call on Sep 28, Bali texted him "Did you hang up on me? Extremely rude and unprofessional." and then "Your name is now blacklisted." He also marked the lead Do Not Contact in Close.

## Round two: what to change

Round one answered the first question: Welcome Series leads pick up. Round two should answer the second: can a setter turn those conversations into closer calls at a rate worth staffing? Six changes, in order of impact:

1. **Book the closer live on every qualified call.** It's the habit behind every closer call Bali booked. Keep the setter call to 5 to 8 minutes: the qualifying questions, then "I have Tuesday at 9 with Harry. Does that work?" Drop the extra intro call on the setter's own calendar: 4 were booked in round one and none has led to a closer call yet.
2. **Leave the pitch to the closer, and answer price in one breath when asked.** Bali spent 6 to 10 minutes pitching before the price, and on lost calls the upfront fee ended the conversation. The setter's answer: "It's a one-time $20,000 fee, paid upfront, with no commission at the end. That's what the hour with Harry covers, and there's no decision on that call."
3. **Call within 5 minutes during staffed hours.** 59% of sign-ups land on weekdays between 8am and 9pm Eastern. Cover that window and call overnight and weekend sign-ups first thing. Leads called within 15 minutes talked 33% of the time and gave half the closer calls. Dial from US local numbers, not the Ontario number Bali uses now.
4. **Three tries in two days, with a voicemail and a text on every miss.** A second try reached people as often as the first, yet 4 in 5 leads got one call. A daily Close view of leads due for try 2 and try 3 makes this automatic.
5. **One approved claims sheet.** Setters say only what leadership has confirmed, starting with the list above.
6. **Track it in Close, with a control group.** Tag every call with an outcome so the tracker fills itself, and hold back 1 in 5 leads from calling to prove the calls add bookings.

Before round two starts, address the Sep 28 texts with Bali and decide whether a manager should reach out to Richard Diaz. Bali marked him Do Not Contact; Richard didn't ask for it.

## Forecast at 1,000 leads a week

At 1,000 Welcome Series leads a week, the way Bali calls today books about 10 closer calls a week. If round two hits its targets, about 25.

| Step | Round one | Round-two target |
|---|---|---|
| New leads called | about half (360 of 743 a week) | all |
| Called within 15 minutes | 10% | 60% |
| Calls per lead | 1.2 | 3, over two days |
| Leads who have a real conversation | 19.5% | 28% |
| Conversations that become a closer call | 5% (6% before cancellations) | 9% |
| Closer calls per 100 leads | 1 | 2.5 |

| Scenario | Leads who talk | Talks that become closer calls | Closer calls a week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan: every lead called the way Bali calls today | 19.5% | 5% | about 10 |
| Round-two target | 28% | 9% | about 25 |
| Upside, not in the plan: every lead reached like the 48 called within 15 minutes | 33% | 19% | about 60 |

- **Where the targets come from.** 28% assumes every unreached lead gets a second try, which reached 17% of people in round one. 9% is round one's 6 closer calls plus the 3 strong buyers lost on the calls above, per 100 conversations.
- **Staffing.** 1,000 leads a week means about 2,500 dials a week once tries 2 and 3 are included. Bali's fullest days were 105 to 110 real calls, so plan on 4 to 5 setters at his pace, fewer with a power dialer.
- **What it could mean in closes.** From January to June 2026, 577 of 3,466 scheduled closer calls closed (17%), at about $17,300 cash each. At 10 closer calls a week that's about 1.7 closes and $29,000 a week; at 25, about 4 closes and $72,000. These are company-wide averages. How these calls show and close isn't known yet, and one of Bali's 6 didn't join.
- **Watch lead quality.** Going from about 740 to 1,000 leads a week may bring in less serious sign-ups. Re-check both rates after the first week.
